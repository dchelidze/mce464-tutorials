Modes as Directions in Configuration Space

A two-DOF system lives in a plane with coordinates \((q_1,q_2)\). The natural frequencies and mode shapes are the special directions where stiffness and inertia point the same way.

1. The eigenproblem as a geometry problem

For the chain wall–\(k_1\)–\(m_1\)–\(k_2\)–\(m_2\)–\(k_3\)–wall, the free motion obeys \(\vb{M}\ddot{\vb{q}}+\vb{K}\vb{q}=\vb{0}\) with $$ \vb{M}=\begin{bmatrix}m_1&0\\0&m_2\end{bmatrix},\qquad \vb{K}=\begin{bmatrix}k_1+k_2&-k_2\\-k_2&k_2+k_3\end{bmatrix}. $$ A harmonic ansatz \(\vb{q}=\vb{\phi}\cos\omega t\) turns this into \((\vb{K}-\omega^2\vb{M})\vb{\phi}=\vb{0}\). Two quadratic forms define the geometry of the plane:

  • kinetic metric \(\vb{q}^{\T}\vb{M}\vb{q}\): its unit level set is an ellipse (the "unit sphere" for the inertia).
  • potential form \(\vb{q}^{\T}\vb{K}\vb{q}\): another family of ellipses.

Move a point \(\vb{q}\) around the unit inertia ellipse. The stiffness ellipse through that point has the normal \(\vb{K}\vb{q}\); the inertia ellipse has the normal \(\vb{M}\vb{q}\). The two normals are parallel, \(\vb{K}\vb{q}=\lambda\vb{M}\vb{q}\), exactly when the two ellipses touch tangentially. Those points are the mode shapes. The multiplier is \(\lambda=\omega^2\).

Two facts to read from the left figure (1) At a mode (the point on a blue or orange line) the plum ellipse is tangent to the ink ellipse and the two arrows align. (2) The tangent to the inertia ellipse at \(\vb{\phi}_1\) is parallel to \(\vb{\phi}_2\). That is orthogonality: \(\vb{\phi}_1^{\T}\vb{M}\vb{\phi}_2=0\) says \(\vb{\phi}_2\) is perpendicular to the normal \(\vb{M}\vb{\phi}_1\), measured with the ordinary dot product. Modes are not perpendicular in the picture unless \(\vb{M}\propto\vb{I}\) (or the masses are uncoupled, \(k_2=0\)).

2. Why a frequency picks a singular matrix

A nonzero \(\vb{\phi}\) with \((\vb{K}-\lambda\vb{M})\vb{\phi}=\vb{0}\) exists only if the matrix is singular. Plot the determinant against \(\lambda\): a parabola for two DOF whose two roots are \(\omega_1^2\) and \(\omega_2^2\).

Characteristic polynomial \(\det(\vb{K}-\lambda\vb{M})\)
The shaded dot slides with your trial \(\lambda\). The matrix is singular only where the curve crosses zero.

3. What a mode looks like in motion

Start from rest at \(\vb{q}(0)=\cos\alpha\,\vb{\phi}_1+\sin\alpha\,\vb{\phi}_2\). The free response is a superposition of two harmonics, $$ \vb{q}(t)=\cos\alpha\,\vb{\phi}_1\cos\omega_1t+\sin\alpha\,\vb{\phi}_2\cos\omega_2t. $$ With \(\alpha=0\) or \(90^\circ\) only one mode is excited: the two masses move in step along a straight line in the \((q_1,q_2)\) plane. For anything in between, the incommensurate frequencies paint a Lissajous figure that fills the parallelogram spanned by the two mode lines.

4. How coupling shapes the frequencies

\(\omega_{1,2}\) versus coupling \(k_2\)
At \(k_2=0\) the masses are uncoupled and the frequencies are \(\sqrt{k_1/m_1}\) and \(\sqrt{k_3/m_2}\) (dotted). Raising the coupling stiffens the chain and widens the gap between the two frequencies.
\(\omega_{1,2}\) versus \(k_1\) (avoided crossing)
The dotted lines are the frequencies of each mass with the other one held fixed, \(\sqrt{(k_1+k_2)/m_1}\) and \(\sqrt{(k_2+k_3)/m_2}\); they cross where the modes veer. With \(k_2>0\) they veer apart, and the mode shapes swap character across the gap.

5. Check yourself

Why is \(\vb{\phi}_1^{\T}\vb{K}\vb{\phi}_2=0\) as well as \(\vb{\phi}_1^{\T}\vb{M}\vb{\phi}_2=0\)?

From \(\vb{K}\vb{\phi}_2=\lambda_2\vb{M}\vb{\phi}_2\) we get \(\vb{\phi}_1^{\T}\vb{K}\vb{\phi}_2=\lambda_2\,\vb{\phi}_1^{\T}\vb{M}\vb{\phi}_2=0\). Geometrically, both ellipses share the same pair of conjugate directions, which is why one change of axes diagonalizes both.

What does a mode shape ratio \(\phi_{2}/\phi_{1}<0\) mean physically?

The two masses move in antiphase: the middle spring stretches the most, and the frequency is higher. The in-phase mode stretches the middle spring less, so it has the lower frequency. (How much less depends on the parameters; only for a symmetric chain is it exactly unstretched.)

If \(k_2=0\), why do the modes become the coordinate axes?

Both \(\vb{M}\) and \(\vb{K}\) become diagonal, so both ellipses have their axes along \(q_1\) and \(q_2\), and the tangency points are the axis intercepts.
